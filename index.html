01 · Overview

What is desertification?

Desertification is land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid regions, driven by climate variation and human activity such as deforestation, overgrazing, unsustainable irrigation and erosion. It is not deserts spreading — it is land losing its productivity. ISRO's satellites map this change over time, and a simple regression model can read the trend and project it forward.

Why it matters: degraded land means lower crop yields, scarcer water and weaker livelihoods. India has pledged to restore 26 million hectares by 2030.

05 · Context

India and desertification: timeline

1994UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) adopted.
1996India ratifies the UNCCD.
2001India's National Action Programme to Combat Desertification is prepared.
2003-05ISRO maps 94.53 million ha as degraded.
2011-13Atlas shows 96.40 million ha degraded.
2014Green India Mission approved to increase forest and tree cover.
2018-19Atlas shows 97.85 million ha degraded — 29.77% of India.
2019India hosts UNCCD COP14 in New Delhi and pledges to restore 26 million ha by 2030.

06 · Drivers

Causes of desertification

Deforestation

Cutting trees removes roots that hold soil and shade that keeps it moist.

Overgrazing

Too many animals strip grass cover, leaving soil exposed to wind and water.

Unsustainable irrigation

Over-pumping groundwater and waterlogging cause salinity and dry land.

Soil erosion

Wind and water erosion carry away fertile topsoil — the largest degradation process in India.

Climate change

Erratic rainfall and rising temperatures stress already dry regions.

07 · Response

What can be done

Plant trees

Afforestation and shelter-belts hold soil and slow the wind. The Green India Mission supports this.

Save water

Drip irrigation and rainwater harvesting cut waste and prevent salinity.

Sustainable farming

Crop rotation, mulching and reduced tillage keep the soil fertile.

Managed grazing

Rotating pastures lets grass regrow instead of being eaten bare.

Satellite monitoring

Satellites and AI spot degradation early, so action is faster and cheaper.

08 · Method

How the analysis works

  1. Data — ISRO-SAC Desertification & Land Degradation Atlas for 2003-05, 2011-13 and 2018-19 (satellite-mapped).
  2. Model — a least-squares linear regression (supervised machine learning), computed live in your browser.
  3. Prediction — the model estimates the degraded area for any year you choose.
  4. Scenario — subtracting restoration effort shows how action changes the future.

Limitations: only three data points; the model ignores rainfall and policy changes, and atlas periods use slightly different mapping methods. Results are indicative.

10 · Significance

Why it matters

  • Threatens food security, water availability and livelihoods.
  • Linked to UN SDG 15.3 (land degradation neutrality), SDG 2 (zero hunger) and SDG 13 (climate action).
  • Remedies: afforestation, sustainable grazing and irrigation, soil and water conservation, public awareness.
